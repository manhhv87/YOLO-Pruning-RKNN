\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports what two real cabbage-field robot runs allow us to
establish, separating what those runs \emph{measure} from what still depends on
hand-labelled ground truth (GT). The pipeline under test is the detector-free and
training-free guidance stack of Section~\ref{sec:method}: an Excess-Green
index~\cite{woebbecke1995,meyer2008} column-peak central-row tracker, a robust
quadratic fit $x=P(y)$ by Huber IRLS~\cite{huber1964}, a look-ahead tangent that
defines the heading $\theta$ together with the cross-track offset $e$, and a
temporal stage built from a sliding-window median, an EMA, and a physical
max-jump gate. The system is never trained, so the two runs serve as a pure test
set. No parameters are fitted on these frames, which removes any path for leakage.

Two boundaries govern every number that follows. Self-consistency is not
correctness: the ``row-found'' rate of Section~\ref{sec:res-reliability} records
only that the tracker \emph{emitted} a guidance line for a frame, and it carries
no claim about whether that line is the correct central row. The jump counts are
likewise descriptive rather than a robustness result. Because the temporal stage
suppresses large frame-to-frame heading jumps as a direct effect of the gate
threshold, the post-fusion jump counts in Section~\ref{sec:res-temporal} follow
arithmetically from that threshold and do not show that the fused heading is more
accurate. The robustness claim that actually matters, heading error against human
GT on the very frames the gate suppresses, paired with the false-reject rate, is
\emph{[TBD pending GT labelling]} and is marked as such in every table.
Cross-track is reported in pixels, as a percentage of the half-image width, and
in centimetres through the image-to-ground homography of
Section~\ref{sec:method:platform}, built from the robot's measured camera
height and tilt.

% =====================================================================
\subsection{Dataset}
\label{sec:res-dataset}
% =====================================================================

The evaluation data are two forward-view video runs recorded by a forward-view
camera on a cabbage field robot, sampled at $1$\,fps and screened to $1482$ forward-view cabbage still frames
at $640\times480$. The two runs cover different sections of the field at different
times of day, so vegetation density and lighting differ between them.
Table~\ref{tab:res-dataset} summarises the runs. The setting is one robot, one
crop, one camera pose, and open-loop logging at $1$\,fps, and the dataset
supports no claim of coverage beyond this narrow operating envelope.

\subsection{Row-emission reliability}
\label{sec:res-reliability}
% =====================================================================

We first assess whether the perception front end emits a guidance line on every
frame. Across all $1482$ frames the Excess-Green index column-peak
tracker emitted a guidance line in $100\%$ of frames ($1482/1482$). To test
whether this figure conceals a silent failure mode in sparse or dense canopy, we
split the frames into vegetation tertiles by per-frame Excess-Green coverage (low,
mid, high). Table~\ref{tab:res-reliability} shows that the emit rate is $100\%$ in
every tertile. This stratification is partly tautological by construction: the tracker locates the
strongest Excess-Green column, so a peak exists whenever any green is present, and
emission across Excess-Green-defined tertiles is close to guaranteed by the design
of the front end. The result therefore shows only that the tracker never aborts.
Whether the canopy density leaves the row correct is the separate accuracy
question of Section~\ref{sec:res-accuracy}.

The $100\%$ row-found rate is a self-consistency statement: in every frame,
including the low-vegetation frames where the canopy signal is weakest, the
pipeline returned a line rather than aborting. Figure~\ref{fig:gallery} overlays
the fitted guidance line on four representative cabbage frames. The rate is not a
correctness statement, since it does not show that the emitted line is the correct
central crop row, only that a line was produced. Whether those lines are right is
an accuracy question that requires human GT, reported (pending) in
Section~\ref{sec:res-accuracy}.

Mapping the per-frame near-row pixel offset through the image-to-ground homography
(Section~\ref{sec:method:platform}) describes the magnitude of the lateral offset
in metric units. The central row sat a median of $1.9$\,cm from the image centre
across all $1482$ frames (mean $5.3$\,cm, 90th percentile $16.3$\,cm), where the
centimetre conversion uses the homography built from the robot's measured camera
pose (Section~\ref{sec:method:platform}) and is reported alongside the
pixel and percentage-of-half-width values. These figures characterise the
\emph{magnitude} of the lateral offset the controller would have observed, a
geometric summary of the runs rather than an error against ground truth. Whether
the estimated offset is \emph{correct} is the separate, pending accuracy question
of Section~\ref{sec:res-accuracy}.

\subsection{Temporal stability}
\label{sec:res-temporal}
% =====================================================================

The temporal stage, a sliding-window median followed by an EMA and a physical
max-jump gate in the spirit of standard recursive smoothing~\cite{kalman1960},
exists to suppress single-frame heading excursions that a controller should not
act on. Table~\ref{tab:res-temporal} reports how many per-frame heading jumps
exceed $10^\circ$, $15^\circ$, and $20^\circ$ in the raw per-frame tracker output
and after the temporal stage. The per-frame jump is the change in heading from the
previous frame and is undefined for the first frame of each run, so the
denominators are $812$ and $670$ as logged.

These counts are descriptive, since the gate suppresses the jumps by design. The
post-fusion counts drop to nearly zero because the max-jump gate rejects exactly
these large frame-to-frame changes. That reduction is an arithmetic property of the
gate threshold rather than an independent measurement of correctness, and it does
not amount to ``jumps eliminated.'' The question that decides whether the temporal
stage helps, namely whether the fused heading is closer to human GT than the raw
heading on the suppressed frames and how often the gate rejects a correct large
heading, is tested in Section~\ref{sec:res-accuracy} and is \emph{[TBD pending GT
labelling]}.

\subsection{Accuracy and ablation evaluation}
\label{sec:res-accuracy}
\label{sec:res-baselines}
% =====================================================================

The quantities on which the deployment claim rests are the accuracy against a
human labelling of the central row and the comparisons that interpret it. Each
requires the hand-labelled GT subset, in which an annotator marks the central
navigation row per frame and the GT line and its look-ahead tangent are derived in
the same $x=P(y)$ parameterisation; inter-annotator agreement then sets a floor on
the smallest meaningful error. That labelling is in progress, so every quantity in
Table~\ref{tab:res-pending} is \emph{[TBD pending GT labelling]}, with the metrics
fixed in advance to keep the evaluation confirmatory. Accuracy is the heading error
$|\theta-\theta^{\mathrm{GT}}|$ in degrees and the cross-track error
$|e-e^{\mathrm{GT}}|$ in pixels, as a percentage of the half-image width, and in
centimetres through the homography of Section~\ref{sec:method:platform}, each
summarised by median, mean, and 90th percentile. The temporal-stage question of
Section~\ref{sec:res-temporal} is tested here as the heading error against GT on
the suppressed large raw-jump frames, raw versus fused, together with the gate's
false-reject rate. The deployed fit is compared against a RANSAC line
fit~\cite{fischler1981} and an equal-weight least-squares fit over the same
Excess-Green column peaks, and the temporal stage is ablated (raw versus
median$+$EMA$+$gate); a self-consistency or jump-count comparison would not
separate these arms, so the discriminating axis is error against the labelled
central row.

The two runs establish that the front end is reliable in the self-consistency
sense, with a line emitted in $100\%$ of $1482$ frames including the
low-vegetation tertile, and they give descriptive context on how often the raw
per-frame heading would have jumped. Accuracy is not yet established. Every
correctness quantity remains \emph{[TBD pending GT labelling]}: heading and
cross-track error against human GT, inter-annotator agreement, error against GT on
the gate-suppressed large raw-jump frames, the false-reject rate, and the baseline and
temporal-ablation comparisons of Table~\ref{tab:res-pending}. Completing the
hand-labelled subset is the one remaining step that turns this from a
self-consistency report into the accuracy validation the deployment claim requires.
