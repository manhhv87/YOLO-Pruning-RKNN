\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On a low-cost field robot without RTK-GPS, the quantities a steering controller actually
consumes are a heading and a cross-track offset, not a detection
score~\cite{cth3,sogaard}. Adopting that interface, this work examines what crop-row
guidance accuracy is achievable with a pipeline that has no trained detector and
no learned weights of any kind. The guidance estimator is assembled entirely from
well-understood stock parts. An Excess-Green index~\cite{woebbecke1995,meyer2008} feeds a
column-peak central-row tracker; a robust quadratic fit $x=P(y)$ by Huber IRLS
($\delta=1.345$, scale $1.4826\cdot\mathrm{MAD}$) models the guidance
line~\cite{huber1964,marchant1995}; the heading $\theta$ is taken as the look-ahead tangent
to $P$ and the cross-track offset $e$ at the vehicle reference row; and a temporal stage of
a sliding-window median, an exponential moving average, and a physical max-jump gate
smooths both signals~\cite{kalman1960}. All components are established. The study offers
an in-field validation of what this stock composition does on a real cabbage robot,
rather than a new method.

Against a $150$-frame human-labelled subset the detector-free pipeline reaches a median heading error of
$5.0^{\circ}$ and a cross-track of about $1$\,cm, with a guidance line emitted on $99.9\%$ of frames. Two findings
stand out. The temporal stage, not the perception front end, carries the robustness: it lowers heading
error on the gate-suppressed frames in both runs, fires selectively on genuinely poor frames, and improves
every front end and index tried, while the fit choice is secondary (Section~\ref{sec:results}). And against
a trained YOLOv5 detector baseline the detector-free pipeline reaches per-frame parity in median heading
(paired difference $0.2^\circ$, $95\%$ interval $[-0.5,1.2]$ spanning zero, and still spanning zero when the
detector is given the same temporal stage) at no labelling or GPU cost; the detector is ahead only in the
error tail and on the wrong-row frames where a vegetation index cannot separate crop from weed -- the
measured cost of going detector-free.

The scope is one robot, one crop, and one camera pose, at $1$\,fps; the claims extend no further. The dataset
and code will be made publicly available upon publication.

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:future-work}
Four steps follow from the boundaries above. An on-robot checkerboard calibration would replace the reused
intrinsics and flat-ground homography with one measured on the cabbage robot. Closing the steering loop from
the pipeline's output, with wheel odometry, would turn the measured guidance accuracy into a realised
intervention rate and expose how controller and terrain dynamics interact with the gate~\cite{rajamani2012}. A second annotator would establish the
inter-annotator agreement floor this single-annotator study lacks. And extending the dataset to further row
crops would test whether the temporal stage remains the robustness lever under genuine distribution shift.
