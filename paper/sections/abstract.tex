\begin{abstract}
    \noindent Vision-only crop-row guidance lets a low-cost field robot steer without RTK-GPS: for every
    frame the steering controller consumes a heading and a cross-track offset, not a detection score. These
    signals are usually produced by a trained row detector, which requires costly field-specific labelling
    and degrades when the canopy or growth stage drifts away from the training distribution. This work asks,
    in the field on a cabbage robot, whether a detector-free and training-free pipeline of classical
    components can deliver the same interface. An Excess-Green column-peak tracker, restricted to a
    $\pm220$\,mm ground safety corridor, locates the central row; a robust quadratic fit models its
    curvature; the heading is taken as the look-ahead tangent and the cross-track as the lateral offset; and
    a temporal stage of a sliding-window median, an exponential moving average, and a physical jump gate
    stabilises both signals. Because no component is trained, the two field runs ($1482$ frames) form a
    leakage-free test set. Against a $150$-frame human-labelled subset the deployed pipeline attains a median
    heading error of $5.0^{\circ}$ and a median cross-track error of $1.1$\,cm, with a residual tail on the
    frames where the vegetation index locks onto a neighbouring row. The temporal stage, not the perception
    choice, is the dominant source of robustness, lowering heading error on the hardest frames across every
    front end and index tested. Against the platform's own trained YOLOv5 detector the pipeline comes within
    about a degree of median heading at zero labelling and GPU cost, the detector remaining more accurate
    chiefly on the wrong-row frames. The study covers one robot, one crop, one camera pose, and open-loop
    operation.
\end{abstract}
