\begin{abstract}
    \noindent Vision-only crop-row guidance lets a low-cost field robot steer without RTK-GPS: for every
    frame the steering controller consumes a heading and a cross-track offset, not a detection score. These
    are usually produced by a trained row detector, which needs costly field-specific labelling and degrades
    as the canopy drifts from its training distribution. This work investigates, on a cabbage field robot,
    whether a detector-free and training-free pipeline of classical components can deliver the same
    interface. An Excess-Green column-peak tracker confined to a ground safety corridor locates the central
    row; a robust quadratic fit models its curvature; the heading is the look-ahead tangent and the
    cross-track its lateral offset; and a temporal stage of a median filter, an exponential moving average,
    and a physical jump gate stabilises both signals. As nothing is trained, the field runs form a
    leakage-free test set. Against a $150$-frame human-labelled subset the pipeline attains a median
    heading error of $5.0^{\circ}$ and a median cross-track error of about $1$\,cm, the temporal stage rather
    than the perception choice being the dominant source of robustness; a residual tail remains where the
    index locks onto a neighbouring row. Against a trained YOLOv5 detector baseline the median heading gap is
    only $0.2^{\circ}$ and not resolvable at this sample size ($95\%$ bootstrap interval $[-0.5,1.2]$), though
    the detector stays uniformly ahead in point estimate and decisively better on those wrong-row frames, at
    zero labelling and GPU cost. The scope is one robot, one crop, and one camera pose.
\end{abstract}
